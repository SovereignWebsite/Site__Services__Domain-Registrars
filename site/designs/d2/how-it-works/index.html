How it works

Five layers sit between you and a working domain. Knowing which one you are dealing with explains most of the prices, the rules and the delays.

The layers

  1. ICANN: coordinates the naming system and contracts with gTLD registries and accredited registrars. It does not control content.
  2. The registry: one organisation in charge of every name under a top-level domain (Verisign for .com, Nominet for .uk, EURid for .eu, the .PT registry for .pt).
  3. The accredited registrar: has direct access to the registry and sells names to the public. Registrars set their own prices.
  4. The reseller: sells on behalf of a registrar. The ICANN pages we fetched do not describe resellers, so we say no more here.
  5. The registrant: the holder of the name. The party the registry data and the transfer rules treat as the owner.

ICANN: "dedicated to keeping the Internet secure, stable and interoperable". For each top-level domain "one company ... is in charge of all domains ending with" it, called the registry, and ICANN contracts with it. An accredited registrar "offers domain name registration services" with "direct access to the gTLD registries". Verisign operates the .com registry and manages relationships with approximately 3,000 accredited registrars. Source: ICANN, what does ICANN do (older page); ICANN, Registrar Accreditation Agreement page; Verisign, the registry. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

The .PT registry now lives at pt.pt (the old dns.pt address redirects there). Source: .PT, domain prices. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

Our reading, not a sourced fact. The .com registry agreement is an ICANN contract, so the ICANN rules and fees below apply to .com and other gTLDs. Country domains such as .uk, .eu and .pt run on their own registry rules. We found no single page stating this, so treat it as a working assumption and check each registry.

WHOIS, RDAP and EPP

WHOIS is the old way to look up who holds a name. RDAP is its replacement, developed by the IETF: ICANN says it was created "as the replacement for the WHOIS protocol" and gives "WHOIS-like registration data". Since 28 January 2025 gTLD registries and registrars are no longer required to provide WHOIS, with exceptions for .com, .name and .post, and all must provide RDAP. Source: ICANN, RDAP. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

RDAP is defined in RFC 7480 to 7484 (HTTP usage, security, query format, JSON responses and bootstrapping). We opened RFC 7480 only. Source: RFC 7480, HTTP usage in RDAP. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

EPP, the Extensible Provisioning Protocol, is how registrars talk to registries: an XML application-layer protocol defined in RFC 5730 (August 2009). The .PT registry requires registrars to integrate through EPP. This is why a registrar API usually wraps EPP rather than replacing it. Source: RFC 5730, Extensible Provisioning Protocol; .PT, becoming a registrar. Checked 25 Nov 2026.
