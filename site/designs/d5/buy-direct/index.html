Can you buy direct?

Short version from the sources: names are sold to the public by registrars, and registries describe themselves as serving registrars. Becoming one yourself is a business with fixed costs, not a way to buy a few domains cheaply.

Does each registry sell to the public, and what does it take to become a registrar?

.PT (.pt)The prices page says registrations are made through registrars, who pay .PT. It does not say whether the public may register directly. Not stated. Source: .PT, domain prices. Checked 25 Nov 2026.€1,200 joining fee on signing the registry-registrar agreement; €1,000 a year after the first year, plus VAT; at least 200 domains under management from the third year; integration through EPP and technical compliance tests; ISO/IEC 27001 or equivalent from 1 January 2029. Source: .PT, becoming a registrar. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

Our reading, not a sourced fact. Adding the ICANN and Verisign figures, an accredited registrar pays $3,500 to apply and $4,000 a year before registering a single name, and must show working capital. Nominet membership is £400 to join and £100 a year, but the wholesale rate is £3.90 against £80.00 listed for non-members, which is why members are the ones who sell. For a handful of customer domains these fixed costs dominate, so using an existing registrar with an API is the practical route. This is arithmetic and judgement on the sourced figures, not a quoted conclusion.

What about the registry's own site?

Some registries have a "Register" or "Find a registrar" link. On the pages we could read, those links lead to the list of accredited registrars. Where a registry page was blocked to our fetcher (Nominet's public pages answered 403), we say so rather than guess. Source: EURid, become a .eu registrar; .PT, domain prices. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

Gaps in this section

  • No registry page we read states outright whether it sells to the public.
  • .eu transfer notes and .pt transfer notes: not stated.
  • ICANN insurance requirement: not stated on the financials page.
  • The .com agreement text itself (the cap clause) could not be read.
