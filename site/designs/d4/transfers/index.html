Transfers, locks, renewals, DNSSEC and privacy

The rules that decide whether you can move a domain, and what happens if you forget to renew it.

Transfers, auth codes and the 60-day rules

ICANN's Transfer Policy (updated 21 February 2024; implementation required by 21 August 2025) applies to gTLDs. Source: ICANN, Transfer Policy. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

  • After a change of registrant, the registrar "must impose a 60-day inter-registrar transfer lock", though the holder may be allowed to opt out before the change request. Source: ICANN, Transfer Policy. Checked 25 Nov 2026.
  • After first registration there is no mandatory lock, but a registrar of record may deny a transfer requested within 60 days of the creation date. A separate 60-day denial ground applies after an inter-registrar transfer. Source: ICANN, Transfer Policy. Checked 25 Nov 2026.
  • The auth code is unique per domain and only identifies the holder; the transfer still needs the process authorisation. If there is no self-service, the registrar must provide the code or remove the transfer lock within five calendar days of the request. Source: ICANN, Transfer Policy. Checked 25 Nov 2026.
  • A registrar must not use transfer processes "as a mechanism to secure payment", with exceptions for non-payment after expiry or for the current period. Source: ICANN, Transfer Policy. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

The .uk change on 9 February 2027

Nominet says the move from the IPS tag change process to Transfer Authorisation Codes will take place on Tuesday 9 February 2027, that a TAC is valid for 14 days, and that "accreditation" replaces "TAGs". Until then the tag-change process is current. Source: Nominet, .uk registry FAQ. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

Not stated. .eu and .pt transfer rules, auth code validity and fees: not stated by any registry page we read. A registrar page (Gandi) gives some notes, shown on its entry.

Renewal notices: ICANN requires a reminder about a month before expiry and another about a week before, and a further notice if the name is not renewed or deleted within five days after expiry. DNS must be interrupted for at least the last eight days a name is renewable. Fees for renewal and redemption must be made reasonably available. Source: ICANN, Expired Registration Recovery Policy; ICANN, about the ERRP (registrants). Checked 25 Nov 2026.

DNSSEC

"DNSSEC strengthens DNS authentication using digital signatures based on public key cryptography." The key material goes to the parent zone, and "in most cases, that communication happens through the zone owner's registrar." Whether a registrar supports it, and automates it, is a column on the comparison. Source: ICANN, DNSSEC: what it is and why it matters. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

Privacy and WHOIS

Since the 2018 Temporary Specification, personal data in public registration data has been redacted; the Registration Data Policy published 21 February 2024 lets a registrar redact where a registrant does not consent to publication. Third-party "privacy services" are a separate, registrar-specific product, compared on the registrars page. Source: ICANN, Registration Data Policy Not read on the official page: from a search summary. Checked 25 Nov 2026.
