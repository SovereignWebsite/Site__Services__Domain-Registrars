Brokers and the aftermarket

If the name you want is free, register it. If it is taken, you have three routes: ask the holder, use a marketplace, or pay a broker. This page explains how each works. It does not recommend any broker, because none has been sourced as one.

When a name is already registered

ICANN says to use ICANN Lookup to find the registrar; the registrar should offer a web form or other method to contact a registrant whose details are not public. Registrant fields in RDAP are often shown as redacted, and the registrar contact must be an email address or web form that does not reveal the registrant's address. Source: ICANN Lookup; ICANN, registrant contact information FAQ Not read on the official page: from a search summary; ICANN, advisory on registrars' contact web forms, May 2026 Not read on the official page: from a search summary. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

A May 2026 ICANN advisory notes the Registration Data Policy took effect on 21 August 2025 and requires registrars to publish a registrant email, often pseudonymised or a web form. At least one registrar's form only allows a message to the registrant or technical contact. Source: ICANN, advisory on registrars' contact web forms, May 2026 Not read on the official page: from a search summary. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

The aftermarket is the resale market for names already registered; a buyer bids or negotiates to move the registration from the current holder. One registrar's guide describes it that way; we found no formal regulation of it in the sources. Source: Network Solutions, domain aftermarket guide Not read on the official page: from a search summary. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

Not stated. Whether a holder must reply: not stated by ICANN. A broker's service page notes a sale can fail if the registrant is unreachable or refuses.

What a broker does

A broker contacts the current registrant, may analyse the name, negotiates a price and helps complete the transaction; some keep the buyer's identity private. This description is from one cloud provider's own service page. Source: Alibaba Cloud, buying a registered domain name Not read on the official page: from a search summary. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

Not stated. Broker fees, and any ICANN or registry page describing brokers: not found. We name no broker.

How escrow works

Escrow.com's own steps: buyer and seller agree the domain and price; the buyer pays Escrow.com; the seller is told to transfer the domain and confirms; the buyer confirms possession and an inspection period runs; funds go to the seller when the transfer is verified or inspection ends. The buyer should verify the seller's ownership before sending funds. Source: Escrow.com, how domain escrow works; Escrow.com, domain name escrow. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

Not stated. Escrow fee amounts and who pays: neither Escrow.com page states them ("low transparent fees" and a calculator).

The transfer step itself follows ICANN's Transfer Policy: the registrar must give the registrant an auth code (or remove the lock) within five calendar days of a request, and a change of registrant name, organisation or email triggers a confirmation process and a 60-day transfer lock unless opted out beforehand. See transfers and renewals. Source: ICANN, transfer policy (registrant page); ICANN, Transfer Policy. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

Expired names, auctions and drop-catching

ICANN: reminders go out about a month and a week before expiry. A registrar may offer an Auto-Renew Grace Period of 1 to 45 days, during which the name may be auctioned by the registrar depending on its terms. After deletion registries must offer a 30-day Redemption Grace Period; then five days of pending delete; then the name is released first come, first served. Source: ICANN, about the ERRP (registrants); ICANN, renewal and expiration FAQ; ICANN, Expired Registration Recovery Policy. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

Nominet (.uk): 0 to 30 days Expiry Grace (the name works and can be renewed); 30 to 90 days Redemption Grace (does not resolve, renewable); 90 to 95 days Pending Delete (not renewable). Names no longer drop at random: each drops at a published UTC time, and a daily drop list is published. Drop catchers are told to use drop lists and EPP availability checks. Source: Nominet, new .uk expiry process and drop lists. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

Verisign (.com): 45 days Auto-Renew Grace and 30 days Redemption, per a registry agreement exhibit seen only in a search summary. Source: Verisign .com registry agreement exhibit (SEC) Not read on the official page: from a search summary. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

One registrar's explainer (for .com): auction houses list expired names between expiry and registry release; backorder services try to register a name the moment it is available, and several backorders on one name usually lead to an auction among them. The same registrar is reported to have stopped taking new backorders on 8 August 2025 (not confirmed on an opened page). Source: GoDaddy, how to secure expired domains; GoDaddy, phasing out domain backorders Not read on the official page: from a search summary. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

Premium names

One registrar explains two kinds: names owned by an existing holder (usually a one-time fee, renewal and transfer at standard rates) and registry premiums, which can carry special pricing for acquisition, renewal and transfer, and may be registered or renewed for one year at a time. Source: Namecheap, what is a premium domain (address no longer resolves, 404 on 25 Nov 2026). Checked 25 Nov 2026.

Not stated. ICANN's own page on registry premium pricing: not found.

Risks

Attackers may try to hijack a domain: the UK NCSC advises using an organisation-controlled registrar account and securing it as it advises for accounts. The NCSC page does not mention scams or fake renewal notices. Source: UK NCSC, protecting your public domain name. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

The Swiss national cyber security centre describes fake messages of the form "someone wants your domain, register it now" and criminals catching lapsed domains, from search snippets only. Source: Swiss NCSC, weekly review 21 (2024) (address no longer resolves, 404 on 25 Nov 2026) Not read on the official page: from a search summary. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

Disputes

If someone holds a name that matches your trademark, the UDRP is the ICANN route: the complainant must show the name is identical or confusingly similar to a mark, that the holder has no rights or legitimate interests, and bad-faith registration and use. The only remedies are cancellation or transfer. Source: ICANN, UDRP. Checked 25 Nov 2026.

For .uk the equivalent is Nominet's Dispute Resolution Service (a complainant needs rights in a similar name and must show an abusive registration), from a search summary because Nominet's pages returned 403. Fees: not stated. Source: Nominet, domain disputes (DRS) Not read on the official page: from a search summary. Checked 25 Nov 2026.
